The analytical arrangements of Bach which I have created began as a desire to understand Bach's compositions. At that point in time I was working on the keyboard work he created known as The French Suites. Up until this point I attempted to understand Bach's works by analyzing the chord progressions of his music and that helped, however I felt that he was doing something that I could see but didn't have the words to express. Then, I had an inspiration to focus on what Bach was doing on the whole number counts. Once I started focusing on the rhythmic aspects of his compositions first and the tonality second, then things fell into place for my sense of relating with what he was doing as a composer. At first, I made one analytical arrangement per composition where I focused on the pitch of the notes which fell upon the whole number counts and made one analytical arrangement per composition. There are six suites of compositions in The French Suites. Each suite has between 6 and 8 compositions. At first I created one analytical arrangement for each composition. Each day I practiced the original composition with its companion analytical arrangement beside it. I would begin from beginning to end until I reached the end of the compositions. 6 suites times 7 compositions each is about 42 compositions plus 42 analytical arrangements, totaling 84 total compositions. It took me several days of practice sessions to read all 84. I did this for about a year. The learning experience was transformative for me. The analytical arrangements I had created captured the essence of the original composition. So, by playing them side by side with the original  I noticed that I could hear the analytical arrangement in my mind while I was sight reading the original. This gave me a doorway into understanding what Bach was doing! I thought since that worked so well, why not make more analytical arrangements based on other divisions of the rhythmic meter and it worked so well that I created 7 analytical arrangements for 1 original composition. This process is quite tedious for a human, especially for a musician who wants to be playing and learning the music, so I turned to Claude and computational musicology to create a computer program to capture the analytical arrangements.
